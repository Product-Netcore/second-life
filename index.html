Everyone wants to do the right thing. Nobody knows where.

The bag of clothes in the corner. The drawer of dead phones. The child's shoes that still fit someone. You'd give them away tomorrow if you knew who would take them, what they refuse, and whether they'll come to your door. That's the whole problem — and it's a search problem, not a goodwill problem.

  • Reuse beats recycle

    A shirt worn by one more person saves 2,700 litres of water compared to making a new one. Listings that redistribute rank above listings that shred.

  • The law is on your side

    India's E-Waste, Battery and Plastic rules make brands responsible for taking their products back. We list the take-back doors most people don't know exist.

  • Verified, dated, honest

    Every phone number and handle comes from the provider's own site, checked on a date you can see. Where no service exists, we say so and tell you the safest thing to do.

  • Near you, in your words

    Choose your city and your language once. The list is only what's reachable from where you are — drop points in your neighbourhood, pickups that cover your pincode.

The kabadi will take your newspaper. He will not take your torn kurta, your dead laptop or your child's shoes — and what he does take, you never learn where it goes.Why this directory exists

What actually happens to what you give

Wearable clothes go to Goonj and reach a village as pay for building a well. A torn saree goes to Respun in Panipat and comes back as fibre. A dead phone goes to a CPCB-authorised recycler that recovers the copper and gold and locks away the lead. Hair becomes a wig. Books get read at ₹10 by a government-school child, then read again. This site exists so each of those journeys starts with one search.

    Every phone number, WhatsApp, email and social handle is copied from the provider's own website or official app-store page — never from a listicle. Each listing shows the date it was last checked. Anything we couldn't confirm is hidden by default and labelled.

Second Lifeसही जगह

Nothing you own has to end in a landfill.

Second Life is a free, independent directory of every NGO, company, brand take-back program and government-approved facility in India that will take your unwanted things — clothes, gadgets, books, shoes, bottles, batteries, vehicles — and give them a second life. We don't collect anything ourselves. We make sure you can find, in one search, who does.

We stand for reuse before recycling, for the informal workers who already do most of India's recycling, and for honesty: every contact is verified against the provider's own site on a date you can see, and where no service exists we say so.
